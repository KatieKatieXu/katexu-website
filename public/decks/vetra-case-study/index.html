WEEK 2 · DECIDE

The strategic fork.

OPTION 1

Keep one integrated product

Find a new mental model that makes three capabilities feel coherent.

  • Preserves the combined technical moat
  • Could create deeper attachment for multi-job users
  • Requires redesign, engineering, recruitment, and repeated testing
  • Feedback remains difficult to attribute
OR
OPTION 2 · CHOSEN

Split into three focused products

Give each audience one clear entrance, promise, and job.

  • Removes discovery complexity immediately
  • Creates cleaner role-specific learning
  • Reduces early-stage development risk
  • Requires three positioning and acquisition strategies
09

AESTHETIC SYSTEM

A polished output can still be wrong.

Audience fitDoes it feel credible and specific to the intended audience?
5-second comprehensionCan someone explain what it is for and what to do next?
Emotional fitDoes the tone serve the purpose and context?
Brand fidelityCould this only come from this brand?
Compositional coherenceDo hierarchy, rhythm, and tone work as one system?
Publish confidenceWould the user send it—and how much must they edit first?

The implementation remains proprietary. This case study shares the evaluation philosophy, not the private aesthetic rulebook or prompts.

REFLECTION

My role was to make the technology understandable enough to learn from.

I did not treat the founder’s technology as the product strategy. I turned first-time confusion into shared evidence, used field research to locate the real problem, made the tradeoff visible, and carried the resulting direction from positioning to interface to production.

Start with friction.Confusion is evidence when it is captured precisely.
Optimize the learning system.Cleaner signals can be more valuable than broader scope.
Use AI for breadth, not accountability.Judgment still owns the decision.
