The argument

Walk into a room.
You feel something before you read anything.

Every interface is a space. Its light, its materials, its noise decide how a person feels before a single feature loads. That feeling isn't decoration on top of the product — for a product people open on their worst days, the feeling is the product. And a feeling can be designed, systematised, and taught to an AI.

Context

Job hunting is a high-anxiety loop.

Rejection, silence, self-doubt — on repeat. And v1 made it worse by promising "auto-apply" and "smart matching": automation the product didn't do. Wrong promise, wrong mood, broken trust.

The design brief, one sentence

Lower cortisol before asking for effort — then deliver dopamine at real moments of progress.

Saved a job → small spark

Finished an analysis → small spark

Moved a card → small spark

Calm first, then a spark. Everything downstream answers to that order.

Method

Teaching the machine to feel the room.

An AI can't be told "make it cozy." It can be shown a space and taught to extract what makes the atmosphere — then hold that as a system while I keep the judgment calls.

Moodboard→ Stylescape→ Style tiles→ Key visual decisions · human→ Tokens & rules→ Build on a new branch
The atmosphere becomes tokens; the taste stays human. Colour ratios, line weight, shadow behaviour, motion rules — extracted, named, enforced.

Function 01 · built on the mood

Every response is information.

Tracking isn't a filing cabinet. Each application you move — and each answer that comes back, a rejection or a next step — is a data point about your standing in the market. The board collects what the market is telling you, one card at a time.

Reflection

Honest about the boundary.

Jobpilot is a coach, not a tool that finds you a job — and the design keeps that honesty structural. In onboarding, one of the four journey arches is drawn in dashed outline: "You apply — out there." The product doesn't pretend to do the part it doesn't do.

The atmosphere argument holds the whole way down: the space sets the mood, the mood carries the emotional value, and the functions — tracking, market position — earn their keep inside that mood, not despite it.
